Idea 1 · Adaptation and mitigation

Exploring how practices may hold up over time

Why

Adaptation pathways are sequences of actions that can be taken step by step as the future unfolds.1,2 They support short-term action while planning under uncertainty, and can show when to adjust a practice and what might come next.

How I see myself contributing
  • Explore tipping points with the agronomy team
  • Help map possible next options3
  • Look at outcomes for different farmers
What it could offer

Place-based pathway: a climate adaptation pathway for floods, droughts, both or other hazards. What services and products would be useful there? What needs to be done if a certain threshold is passed?

Proximity-focused pathway: different futures, combining Proximity's strategy and business model with climate scenarios and socio-economic trends. This could support decisions on which services to introduce, which to park, and which areas to start thinking about.

Perhaps one small pathway for Proximity's decision-making.

Gains

Less burning, nitrogen and smoke

Offsets

Methane from straw

Measure

Soil carbon and local conditions
Field trial

In the Philippines, incorporated straw gave 8.0 t CO₂e/ha/yr, against 4.9 t when burned.2

Meta-analysis

Burning emitted less than full incorporation, yet its other harms are unacceptable.3

Long term

Long-term straw effects on methane were 48% below IPCC estimates.4

Soil carbon

Saturation cuts its projected climate role by 53–81%,5 and it's hard to measure.6

No-Burn's benefits for farmers are inspiring, and I'd love to learn how Proximity approaches the carbon side.
